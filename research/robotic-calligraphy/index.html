From Stroke Data to Ink Marks: Exploring Human Participation in Robotic Calligraphy

An XArm850 prototype for studying how stroke mapping and brush-pressure control shape robotic Chinese calligraphy.

Workflow from Hanzi Writer stroke data to coordinate mapping, Z-axis pressure planning, robotic brush execution, and manual review.

Research Motivation

Robotic calligraphy often focuses on reproducing a target character. This project examines where human judgment can enter the transformation from digital stroke data to physical ink marks. We focus on the intermediate stages of writing, including stroke representation, coordinate mapping, brush pressure, and revision.

System and Approach

We built a prototype with an XArm850, a Chinese brush, ink, and xuan paper. For regular script, the system reads ordered stroke medians from Hanzi Writer, maps and resamples them in the robot workspace, and executes each stroke in sequence. Stroke types are paired with Z-axis pressure profiles to control brush contact and stroke width.

Human Participation

In the current prototype, we inspect each written character and adjust stroke paths, type overrides, pressure profiles, speed, and scale between writing trials. These controls operate at the configuration level; a future version could expose selected parameters through an interactive interface.

My Contributions

As team leader and first author, I led the research framing and co-developed the prototype with my teammate. My contributions included iterative system testing, documenting the pipeline, and analyzing which parameters could support future human control.
